Items marked "(metadata only)" were found in OpenAlex but their full text was not read in this session. Items marked "(from background knowledge, not verified this session)" have no fetched source and should be checked before citing in a final report.

Project facts used below (from the repo's `docs/DEVELOPMENT.md` and the assignment): score S(x) = (1/k) μ·x − (λ/k²) xᵀΣx with Σx = k, swap (Kawasaki) moves, 4,368 states at k = 5, n = 16, exact transition matrices and spectral gaps are planned, all 64 quarterly windows are single-peaked, and linear terms are about 13 times larger than covariance terms.

## 1. Which ruggedness metrics predict slow Metropolis mixing on small (exactly enumerable) problems?

### Takeaway
At 4,368 states every metric can be computed exactly, so use the spectral gap of the actual swap-Metropolis matrix at the benchmark temperature as the primary difficulty measure. Explain it with three cheap landscape statistics: (a) the number of local optima under swap moves and the share of states in the global optimum's basin, (b) the lowest barrier you must climb to leave the second-best funnel and reach the global optimum (from a disconnectivity graph or local optima network), and (c) the score gap between the best and second-best optima. Weinberger autocorrelation length and fitness-distance correlation are weak predictors for this objective class and should be reported only as context.

### Cited Findings
- **Spectral gap as the hardness measure.** Layden et al. built the full 2ⁿ × 2ⁿ transition matrix and took δ = 1 − max |λ| over eigenvalues other than 1, for random Sherrington-Kirkpatrick (SK) instances with 3 ≤ n ≤ 10 (500 instances per n). At T = 1 they fit average δ ∝ 2^(−kn) with k = 0.264 (quantum), 0.94 (local single flip), 0.948 (uniform) and 0.76 (a "mismatched" quantum control). They note that computing δ is "much more demanding" than running MCMC, which is feasible only at small n. — [Layden et al. 2023, Nature, arXiv:2203.12497](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2203.12497); DOI [10.1038/s41586-023-06095-4](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06095-4)
- **Where the quantum advantage appears.** Layden et al. report the advantage at low temperature (T ≲ 1). There the landscape is rugged, local moves rarely leave a minimum, and uniform moves are almost always rejected. Their quantum proposals combined small |ΔE| with large Hamming distance. At high T the uniform proposal already mixes well. — [Layden et al., arXiv:2203.12497](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2203.12497)
- **Small-size results can mislead.** Orfi and Sels argue that the empirical speedup in Layden et al. was measured on fewer than ten spins, in a problem with large finite-size effects. They derive gap bounds that rule out a speedup for unital quantum proposals on worst-case unstructured sampling. A follow-up says the speedup requires "fine-tuned quenches." — [Orfi & Sels, arXiv:2403.03087](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2403.03087), PRA DOI [10.1103/physreva.110.052414](https://doi.org/10.1103/physreva.110.052414); [Orfi & Sels, arXiv:2408.07881](https://web3.arxiv.org/pdf/2408.07881)
- **Weinberger autocorrelation.** This is the standard random-walk correlation measure of how "correlated" a landscape is. — [Weinberger 1990, Biol. Cybern., DOI 10.1007/bf00202749](https://doi.org/10.1007/bf00202749) (metadata only, 563 citations)
- **Autocorrelation length and the number of local optima.** The autocorrelation-length conjecture (Angel & Zissimopoulos) says a larger correlation length means fewer local optima. Graph bipartitioning, whose neighborhood is the Johnson graph, is listed as an "elementary landscape," meaning its objective is an eigenvector of the graph Laplacian. — [Chicano, landscape theory slides](https://neo.lcc.uma.es/staff/francis/pdf/gecco10-chicano.pdf); [Stadler group abstract list](https://www.tbi.univie.ac.at/papers/Abstracts/92-01-002abs.html)
- **Graph bipartitioning tested directly.** Krakhofer and Stadler simulated the number of local minima in graph bipartitioning and explained it through the landscape's correlation length. The abstract gives no numbers. — [Krakhofer & Stadler, "Local Minima in the Graph Bipartitioning Problem"](https://www.tbi.univie.ac.at/papers/Abstracts/96-01-02.html)
- **Fitness-distance correlation (FDC).** — [Jones & Forrest 1995, ICGA](https://api.openalex.org/works?search=Fitness%20distance%20correlation%20as%20a%20measure%20of%20problem%20difficulty) (metadata only, 509 citations). It has been critiqued as an imperfect difficulty measure. — [Collard et al. 1998 "FDC revisited"](https://api.openalex.org/works?search=Fitness%20Distance%20Correlation%20revisited) (metadata only)
- **Surveys of landscape metrics.** Malan and Engelbrecht survey the techniques (ruggedness, neutrality, deception, FDC, autocorrelation, information content, basins). — [Malan & Engelbrecht 2013, Information Sciences, DOI 10.1016/j.ins.2013.04.015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ins.2013.04.015) (metadata only). A later update is [Malan 2021, Algorithms, DOI 10.3390/a14020040](https://doi.org/10.3390/a14020040) (metadata only).
- **Local optima networks (LONs).** Nodes are local optima and edges are transitions under a given operator. In Ochoa and Veerapen's TSP studies, most instances showed multiple funnels, and "the number, disposition and interaction of these funnels seem to relate to search difficulty." A global optimum in a small, narrow funnel makes search harder. — [Ochoa & Veerapen, J. Heuristics 2018, DOI 10.1007/s10732-017-9334-0](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s10732-017-9334-0); [Ochoa et al. GECCO 2016](https://storre.stir.ac.uk/bitstream/1893/23046/1/GECCO2016-adsfcl%20%281%29.pdf); [Ochoa, Vérel, Daolio, Tomassini 2014, LON chapter, DOI 10.1007/978-3-642-41888-4_9](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-41888-4_9) (metadata only)
- **Funnels and disconnectivity graphs.** These come from chemical-physics energy landscapes. The 38-atom Lennard-Jones cluster's double funnel is the classic example of a landscape where the global minimum sits in the narrower funnel and thermal sampling is slow. — [Doye, Miller & Wales 1999, J. Chem. Phys., DOI 10.1063/1.478595](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.478595) (metadata only). Folding funnels have also been studied in spin systems. — [Garstecki, Hoang & Cieplak 1999, PRE, DOI 10.1103/physreve.60.3219](https://doi.org/10.1103/physreve.60.3219) (metadata only)
- **PT mixing time as a hardness measure.** For engineered Chimera spin glasses, the parallel tempering mixing time τ tracked solver time-to-solution as τ ∼ t_HFS^1.4. Instances with τ > 10⁷ occurred in about 2 per 10⁴ random instances. — [Marshall, Martin-Mayor & Hen 2016, arXiv:1605.03607](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1605.03607)

### Inferences
- **A linear objective has one optimum.** On the k-subset space with swap moves, a purely linear objective (score = Σ cᵢxᵢ) has exactly one local optimum, the top-k set, up to ties. Any non-top-k set has a held asset i and an unheld asset j with cⱼ > cᵢ, so swapping them improves the score. The project's finding that all 64 windows are single-peaked when linear terms are about 13 times larger is therefore expected, not a data accident. Ruggedness can only come from the off-diagonal Σ terms being comparable to differences in the effective linear field, defined in the next bullet.
- **Variance terms act as a linear field.** Because xᵢ² = xᵢ for 0/1 variables, the diagonal of Σ joins the linear term. The effective field is hᵢ = μᵢ/k − λΣᵢᵢ/k². A useful dimensionless control parameter is r = (λ/k²) × (spread of the off-diagonal Σᵢⱼ over pairs) / (spread of hᵢ across assets). Expect multiple optima only once r is roughly order one or larger. Reporting r per window is cheaper and more interpretable than the current "13×" magnitude ratio. The relevant quantity is spread, not mean, because the uniform part of Σ is constant under the cardinality constraint (see Section 3).
- **Autocorrelation length is nearly fixed by structure here.** A quadratic function on a Johnson graph decomposes into a few elementary components (the linear part is one component, and the quadratic part adds at most two more), so Weinberger's correlation length moves only within a narrow, analytically constrained range. It will separate "mostly linear" from "mostly quadratic" instances but will not rank instances well by mixing time. (Inference from the elementary-landscape framing in the Chicano and Stadler sources above. The exact decomposition for k-subset quadratic functions was not verified in this session.)
- **Recommended metric stack for n = 16, k = 5.** All are exact and run in seconds.
  1. Spectral gap δ(T) of the swap-Metropolis matrix, swept over T, plus the relaxation time 1/δ. This is the ground truth.
  2. Number of strict swap-local optima, the global optimum's basin share (fraction of states whose steepest-ascent path ends there), and the global-to-second optimum score gap ΔS.
  3. Barrier height B: the minimum over paths from the second-best optimum to the global optimum of the largest score drop along the path. Compute it with a union-find sweep over states sorted by score, which also gives the disconnectivity graph. At low T, Arrhenius or Freidlin-Wentzell reasoning predicts 1/δ grows like exp(B/T) (standard large-deviation result, not re-verified this session). B/T is likely the single best scalar predictor of slow swap mixing.
  4. A LON with escape edges (perturb with 2 swaps, then local search) to count funnels.
  5. FDC and autocorrelation length as secondary context only.
- **Overlap distribution and replica-symmetry breaking.** These are thermodynamic-limit concepts. At n = 16, the analogous diagnostic is the Boltzmann-weighted overlap |x ∩ x′| between samples at the benchmark T. A bimodal overlap histogram means two well-separated low-score clusters, which is the same information as a two-funnel LON.
- **Choose T by the gap, not by convention.** If the chain is effectively at T → 0, the gap is dominated by B. If T is far above B, every instance mixes fast. The interesting window is T comparable to B and to ΔS. Layden et al. found the quantum advantage only at low T.

### Gaps
- I found no paper that benchmarks LON or funnel metrics against exact spectral gaps on k-subset or Johnson-graph landscapes. The predictive ranking above is an inference.
- The exact Weinberger correlation length formula for graph bisection or quadratic k-subset functions was not retrieved (only the Happel & Stadler 1992 abstract listing).
- Frustration-index results were not found in an MCMC-hardness context. The OpenAlex hits were pure graph theory.

## 2. Constrained landscapes and instance-construction or tuning techniques

### Takeaway
Fixed-cardinality quadratic problems such as graph bisection, densest-k-subgraph and cardinality-constrained QUBO are classic NP-hard, rugged landscapes even with all-positive couplings, because the constraint itself creates frustration. The planting literature (frustrated loops, Wishart planted ensemble, tile planting) shows hardness is tunable through a single density-type parameter with an easy-hard-easy peak. Adaptive instance search (mutate the instance, keep changes that slow the chain) raises hardness by orders of magnitude.

### Cited Findings
- **Frustrated-loop planting (Hen et al. 2015).** Instances are sums of random loops. Each loop has all-ferromagnetic couplings except one antiferromagnetic coupling, so the planted state minimizes every loop simultaneously and the ground state is known. Hardness is controlled by clause density α = (number of loops)/(number of spins), with an easy-hard-easy peak near a critical α. The authors found no speedup on the hardest instances. — [Hen et al. 2015, PRA 92 042325, arXiv:1502.01663](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1502.01663); DOI [10.1103/physreva.92.042325](https://doi.org/10.1103/physreva.92.042325)
- **Drawback of loop planting.** The range of coupler values is uncontrolled and grows with system size. — summarized via [Hamze et al. 2018, arXiv:1711.04083](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1711.04083) (search-snippet level)
- **Wishart planted ensemble (WPE), construction.** Take an N × M matrix W with M = αN Gaussian columns constrained to Wᵀt = 0, where t is the planted state. Set J = −(1/N)WWᵀ with the diagonal zeroed. The energy is then proportional to ‖Wᵀs‖² up to a constant, so t is a ground state. — [Hamze, Raymond, Pattison, Biswas, Katzgraber 2020, PRE 101 052102, arXiv:1906.00275](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1906.00275); DOI [10.1103/physreve.101.052102](https://doi.org/10.1103/physreve.101.052102)
- **WPE, hardness profile.**
  - Easy-hard-easy in α. At N = 32, optimized parallel tempering peaked at M = 3 (α = 3/32 ≈ 0.094), taking about 55 minutes per instance.
  - Large α tends toward a Curie-Weiss ferromagnet, which is easy.
  - For α < 1 the paramagnet is locally stable at every T > 0, a "deceptive dynamical trap" that simulated annealing and PT fall into. There is a first-order transition, for example T_c ≈ 0.065 at α = 0.5 and 0.335 at α = 1.0.
  - At fixed numerical precision, exponentially many near-ground states appear, so M must grow with N for true hardness.
  - Source: [Hamze et al., arXiv:1906.00275](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1906.00275)
- **Tile planting.** The problem graph is partitioned into edge-disjoint frustrated subgraphs, planting a known ground state. This gives "a wide range of tunable hardness" with multiple hardness transitions, measured with population annealing, simulated annealing and simulated quantum annealing. — [Perera, Hamze, Raymond, Weigel, Katzgraber 2020, PRE 101 023316, arXiv:1907.10809](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1907.10809); DOI [10.1103/physreve.101.023316](https://doi.org/10.1103/physreve.101.023316)
- **Posiform planting** is a further QUBO planting method. — [Hahn, Pelofske, Djidjev 2023, Front. Comput. Sci., DOI 10.3389/fcomp.2023.1275948](https://doi.org/10.3389/fcomp.2023.1275948) (metadata only)
- **A recent critique of planting.** A 2026 audit argues that planted benchmarks can leak structure and pose verification hazards. — [Li 2026, IEEE Access, DOI 10.1109/access.2026.3738535](https://doi.org/10.1109/access.2026.3738535) (metadata only, title-level)
- **Adaptive hard-instance engineering.** Random Adaptive Optimization flips a random coupling's sign and accepts the flip if time-to-solution rises, otherwise with probability exp(−β|ΔTTS|).
  - After 500 steps, instances were about 10× harder than random. The hardest was about 250× harder than a typical random instance.
  - The method was about 100× more efficient than screening random instances for high PT mixing time.
  - A planted-solution variant (swapping frustrated loops) was less effective: mean TTS ratio about 4.3 after 2,000 steps.
  - Source: [Marshall, Martin-Mayor & Hen 2016, arXiv:1605.03607](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1605.03607)
- **Classic benchmark warnings.** Spin glasses on Chimera have only a zero-temperature transition and may be "blind" to quantum speedup. Benchmarks must have nontrivial finite-temperature structure. — [Katzgraber, Hamze & Andrist 2014, PRX 4 021008, arXiv:1401.1546](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.1546v1). See also [Katzgraber et al. 2015, "Seeking quantum speedup through spin glasses: the good, the bad, and the ugly," PRX, DOI 10.1103/physrevx.5.031026](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevx.5.031026) (metadata only).
- **Graph bisection on the Johnson graph.** It is an elementary landscape, and its number of local minima has been related to correlation length. — [Chicano slides](https://neo.lcc.uma.es/staff/francis/pdf/gecco10-chicano.pdf); [Krakhofer & Stadler](https://www.tbi.univie.ac.at/papers/Abstracts/96-01-02.html). Johnson et al.'s simulated annealing study on graph partitioning is the classic experimental reference. — [Johnson, Aragon, McGeoch, Schevon 1989, Oper. Res., DOI 10.1287/opre.37.6.865](https://doi.org/10.1287/opre.37.6.865) (metadata only)
- **Graph partitioning as a spin glass at fixed magnetization.** Fu and Anderson 1986 (J. Phys. A 19, 1605) mapped graph partitioning onto a spin glass with fixed total magnetization. — (from background knowledge, not verified this session)

### Inferences (how each knob should move optima count and gap)
- **Ratio of quadratic to linear terms (λ, or shrinking μ).** This is the main knob. With a near-zero spread of hᵢ the problem becomes pure cardinality-constrained minimum variance, which is a densest or sparsest-k-subgraph-type quadratic problem. As r rises past about 1, expect the optima count to jump from 1 to many and δ at fixed T to fall. Sweep r continuously and plot optima count, B and δ(T).
- **Sign mixing and frustration.** In 0/1 variables at fixed k, a positive Σᵢⱼ is a pairwise repulsion: it penalizes holding both i and j. Even all-repulsive heterogeneous couplings are frustrated under the cardinality constraint, as in graph bisection. Adding negative correlations (hedges) creates attractive pairs, and mixing attractive and repulsive pairs generates competing clusters, the multi-funnel structure LON studies link to difficulty.
- **Planting, adapted to fixed k.** The WPE has a direct finance reading. J = −WWᵀ/N is a negated factor-model covariance, and the planted state has zero exposure to all M factors. A k-subset analogue is: "find the k-asset equal-weight portfolio with zero exposure to M risk factors," with Σ = WWᵀ + D and loadings constructed so a chosen k-subset is exactly factor-neutral. The WPE's α = M/N maps to the number of risk factors per asset. Their hardness peak at small α (3 factors at N = 32) suggests trying M = 1 to 3 factors at n = 16. The WPE is a ±1 model without a cardinality constraint, so this adaptation is untested.
- **Adaptive search on finance-legitimate parameters.** The Marshall et al. procedure, used to maximize 1/δ at the benchmark T, could perturb things like λ, the window, the universe or the shrinkage. It must be applied symmetrically: selecting instances that are hard for swap-Metropolis biases against that baseline. Report the selection rule and test the selected instances against all baselines in Section 4.
- **Sparse versus dense couplings.** The planting and Chimera results suggest that sparse, low-dimensional coupling graphs tend to have simple finite-T structure. Dense, SK-like couplings are where Layden et al. found gap differences. A 16-asset covariance is naturally dense, which is favorable.
- **Temperature.** Hardness is only defined relative to T. A WPE-style first-order transition, or a trap at low T, means a fixed T can make an instance look trivially easy (high T) or impossible for everyone (very low T). Report δ(T) curves and pick T where the classical gap is small but not machine-zero.
- **Higher-order terms.** Higher-order terms (for example, coskewness penalties or a nonlinear risk measure) add multi-body couplings and break the elementary-landscape structure of quadratic functions. Expect more optima, but also more expensive quantum phase circuits. No source in this session quantifies this for k-subset problems.

### Gaps
- No paper found that plants solutions in fixed-cardinality (Johnson-graph) QUBOs specifically. All planting results above are for unconstrained ±1 Ising models.
- The analytic WPE hardness-peak formula was in the unread second half of the paper.
- No quantitative k-subset results were retrieved for densest-k-subgraph, quadratic knapsack or k-median landscapes (OpenAlex searches for quadratic-knapsack landscapes returned nothing relevant).

## 3. How these map back to financial data choices (market mode, RMT cleaning, spin-glass portfolio literature)

### Takeaway
Econophysics shows that portfolio optimization with a nonlinear constraint has exponentially many near-optimal, very different portfolios (Galluccio, Bouchaud & Potters 1998), and that the no-short-selling version has a spin-glass-like landscape with disorder chaos (Garnier-Brun et al. 2021). The project's planned "remove the market mode step by step" dial is weaker than it looks. Under a fixed cardinality the uniform part of the market mode is a constant and has no effect on the landscape, so removing it changes ruggedness only through beta heterogeneity. Better knobs are the risk-to-return ratio, the noisiness of Σ (window length versus RMT cleaning) and the universe composition (hedging assets).

### Cited Findings
- **Galluccio, Bouchaud & Potters 1998, setup.**
  - The abstract states: "the number of 'optimal' solutions is generically exponentially large: rationality is thus de facto of limited use."
  - The model has a nonlinear margin constraint Σ γWᵢ|pᵢ| = wealth (short and long positions allowed).
  - Optimality gives Sᵢ = sign[Hᵢ + Σⱼ JᵢⱼSⱼ] with J ∝ C⁻¹. These are the TAP or metastable states of a zero-temperature spin glass, and risk plays the energy.
  - The count of locally optimal sign patterns grows like exp(N f(a)). The parameter a measures the diagonal (idiosyncratic) share of C, with f(1) ≈ 0.686, f → ln 2 for large a, and f → 0 as a → 0.
  - Source: [arXiv:cond-mat/9801209](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/cond-mat/9801209); Physica A 259:449, DOI [10.1016/s0378-4371(98)00332-x](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0378-4371(98)00332-x)
- **Galluccio, Bouchaud & Potters 1998, empirical results.**
  - NYSE data (406 stocks, 1991–96) fit a ≈ 0.34. Empirical f ≈ 0.68 versus theory 0.63 on NYSE subsets, and 0.43 versus 0.32 for 172 London stocks. The authors flag strong finite-size effects.
  - With 100 US stocks, an optimal portfolio can be formed in "roughly 10²⁹ ways." Near-degenerate solutions differ greatly in composition and can reorder after small changes to the matrix.
  - Large eigenvalues, including Lévy-like broad coupling spectra, suppress the number of metastable states.
  - The paper does not explicitly discuss the market mode.
  - Source: [ar5iv cond-mat/9801209](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/cond-mat/9801209)
- **Garnier-Brun, Benzaquen, Ciliberti, Bouchaud 2021 (no short selling).** Optimal long-only portfolios are equivalent to Lotka-Volterra equilibria with a rank-one interaction matrix. The log of the average number of solutions grows as N^α with α ≤ 2/3. The authors write that "the solution landscape is similar to that of spin-glasses, i.e. very different configurations are quasi-degenerate," with "disorder chaos." — [arXiv:2104.00668](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.00668); J. Stat. Mech., DOI [10.1088/1742-5468/ac21d9](https://doi.org/10.1088/1742-5468/ac21d9)
- **Andrecut 2013** maps nonlinearly constrained portfolio optimization to ground states of a long-range Ising spin glass with covariance-derived couplings. — [arXiv:1311.2511](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1311.2511); DOI [10.1016/j.physleta.2013.12.013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physleta.2013.12.013) (abstract-level)
- **Ciliberti, Kondor & Mézard** study portfolio feasibility under expected shortfall with statistical-mechanics methods (a phase transition in estimation). — [DOI 10.1201/9781420081923.ch14](https://doi.org/10.1201/9781420081923.ch14) (metadata only)
- **RMT noise cleaning.** Most eigenvalues of empirical financial correlation matrices are consistent with pure noise (Marchenko-Pastur). Only the few largest, including the market mode, carry information. — [Laloux, Cizeau, Bouchaud, Potters 1999, PRL 83:1467, DOI 10.1103/physrevlett.83.1467](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevlett.83.1467) (metadata only, 1,285 citations); [Plerou et al. 2002, PRE 65:066126, DOI 10.1103/physreve.65.066126](https://doi.org/10.1103/physreve.65.066126) (metadata only)
- **Removing the market mode.** Standard practice regresses returns on the market (CAPM style) and keeps the residuals. A data-derived factor removes correlation better than an exogenous index such as the S&P 500. — [Borghesi, Marsili, Miccichè, arXiv:physics/0702106](https://arxiv.org/pdf/physics/0702106) (search-snippet level). In a São Paulo study, regression-based market removal helped forecasts more often than RMT cleaning. — [arXiv:1201.0625](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1201.0625) (search-snippet level)
- **Hierarchical structure.** Correlation-based hierarchies and clusters (sectors) sit beneath the market mode. — [Mantegna 1999, EPJ B, DOI 10.1007/s100510050929](https://doi.org/10.1007/s100510050929); [Tumminello, Lillo, Mantegna 2010, DOI 10.1016/j.jebo.2010.01.004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jebo.2010.01.004) (metadata only)
- **No direct link found.** No source was found that connects market-mode removal to the number of local optima or frustration in a portfolio spin-glass model. — [search result summary](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1311.2511)

### Inferences (evaluation of the "difficulty dial" and better knobs)
- **Why the market-mode dial is weak.**
  - Write Σ = σ²_m ββᵀ + Σ_res. For equal-weight k-subsets the market part contributes (λ/k²)σ²_m(β·x)².
  - If all betas were equal, β·x = kβ̄ for every valid portfolio, so the market mode adds a constant and has no effect on the landscape.
  - Only beta dispersion matters. It acts like a rank-one "low-beta preference" term and is ferromagnetic-like (it favors a coherent low-beta cluster), so it is not a source of many optima.
  - Removing the market mode therefore mainly rescales the off-diagonal spread. It does not by itself create frustration, and the score gap from μ stays about 13× larger. Expect the dial to leave most windows single-peaked unless λ or the μ spread changes too.
  - Rank-one matrices are also the special case Garnier-Brun et al. analyze for the unconstrained long-only problem, where multiplicity comes from the positivity constraint, not from the matrix.
- **Better knob 1: risk-return balance.** Increase λ, equivalently shrink μ toward its cross-sectional mean, for example with James-Stein or Bayes-Stein shrinkage, or set μ to 0 for the cardinality-constrained minimum-variance problem. This is the most credible finance story ("a highly risk-averse investor, or one who distrusts noisy mean estimates"), and it directly controls r from Section 1. The minimum-variance case is also a well-studied problem in practice.
- **Better knob 2: estimation noise.** Galluccio et al. tie multiplicity to the random-matrix part of C. Shorter estimation windows (raising q = n/T toward 1) or uncleaned sample Σ inject Marchenko-Pastur noise eigenvectors with random-sign loadings, which makes couplings more SK-like and more frustrated. RMT cleaning or shrinkage (Ledoit-Wolf) does the opposite and smooths the landscape. A defensible dial is the estimation window length or the shrinkage intensity. Its finance meaning: the landscape the optimizer sees is rugged because of estimation noise, which is the Galluccio "limited rationality" message.
- **Better knob 3: universe composition.** Add ETFs that create sign-mixed residual correlations: long-duration Treasuries versus equities, gold, USD, volatility or inverse ETFs, and sector pairs with opposing factor exposure. These create attractive (hedge) and repulsive (redundant) pairs, which is multi-funnel structure. It is realistic for multi-asset ETF allocation.
- **Better knob 4: residual-correlation objective.** Use the residual (market-removed) correlation matrix together with heavier risk weight, so the objective is "pick 5 diversified, mutually uncorrelated sector bets." This is close to the max-diversification / densest-k-subgraph structure that is known to be NP-hard and rugged.
- **Better knob 5: planted factor-neutral portfolio.** Use the WPE-style construction from Section 2. It is synthetic, but the factor-model interpretation is credible. It gives a known optimum and a single hardness parameter (factors per asset).
- **Keep the market-mode dial as an ablation.** It answers "does removing the dominant eigenvector matter?" and the prediction is that it mostly does not. Pair it with λ, window length and universe sweeps, and report optima count, B and δ for each setting.

### Gaps
- No paper found that computes the number of local optima for cardinality-constrained (k-of-n, equal-weight) mean-variance portfolios as a function of correlation structure. The Galluccio and Garnier-Brun results are for continuous weights with sign or positivity constraints.
- The Bouchaud-Potters book chapters on spin-glass portfolios and the Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage papers were not fetched.

## 4. Classical baselines needed for a credible QeMCMC advantage claim

### Takeaway
A fair comparison must hold the target distribution, temperature and attempt budget fixed. Classical baselines should cover (i) local swap Metropolis, (ii) uniform or multi-swap proposals, (iii) informed heat-bath proposals, (iv) simulated annealing and parallel tempering (replica exchange) as standard methods for rugged landscapes, (v) population annealing, (vi) tabu search or greedy restarts as the practical portfolio heuristic, and (vii) exact solvers (enumeration, MIQP) as ground truth. Layden et al.'s experience suggests cluster moves add little at this size, and Orfi and Sels' bounds mean any small-n advantage must be framed carefully.

### Inferences
- **Baselines already planned in DEVELOPMENT.md §5.1:** swap Metropolis, multi-swap, heat-bath, greedy with restarts, simulated annealing, and classically sampling the ideal quantum table.
- **Baselines to add:**
  - Parallel tempering with swap moves, at the same total attempt budget summed across replicas. This is the standard method for rugged landscapes and the most likely to erase a gap advantage.
  - A uniform random k-subset proposal, the analogue of Layden's "uniform" baseline. It is cheap and essential, because at n = 16 and k = 5 a uniform jump reaches any of 4,368 states.
  - Tabu search, as the classic cardinality-constrained-portfolio heuristic.
  - Optionally, population annealing.
- **Cluster moves.** Houdayer and Swendsen-Wang do not preserve cardinality without modification, and Layden et al. saw little benefit. A Kawasaki-compatible cluster move (swap a correlated group in and out) is optional.
- **Metric hygiene.** Compare spectral gaps and attempts-to-optimum at matched T. For PT, report gaps per replica and the round-trip time.
- **Avoid a selection-bias trap.** If instances are tuned to make swap-Metropolis slow (Section 2), also report how PT and uniform proposals do on the same instances. Otherwise the instances are cherry-picked against one baseline.
- **Scaling caveat.** Present any n = 16 gap ratio as a fixed-size proposal-efficiency result, not a scaling claim, citing Orfi & Sels. Back it up with the 6-of-20 (38,760-state) benchmark to show a trend.
