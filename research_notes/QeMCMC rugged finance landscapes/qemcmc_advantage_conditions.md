# When QeMCMC beats classical Metropolis: landscape conditions and cardinality-constrained variants

Notes compiled 2026-11-14. Discovery: OpenAlex citation trace from Layden et al. 2023 (OpenAlex W4384009058; 104 citing works indexed when checked), plus OpenAlex keyword search and web search. The Layden paper was read in full (arXiv PDF). The other papers were mostly read at abstract level through OpenAlex. Two (Marshall et al. 2026 and Kawamata & Fujii 2026) were read in full HTML. Where a claim rests only on an abstract, that is noted.

## Q1: What Layden et al. 2023 showed, and why the advantage appears

### Takeaway
Layden et al. measured the exact absolute spectral gap δ for n = 3 to 10 spins on fully connected SK-like instances with random fields. At T = 1 the quantum proposal's δ scaled as about 2^(−0.264n), against about 2^(−0.94n) for single-spin-flip proposals, a fitted "cubic/quartic" enhancement. The advantage is concentrated at low temperature (T ≲ 1) and on frustrated instances whose lowest energies include several near-degenerate local minima. It comes from proposals that are far in Hamming distance but small in |ΔE|.

### Cited Findings
- Bibliographic record: D. Layden, G. Mazzola, R. V. Mishmash, M. Motta, P. Wocjan, J.-S. Kim, et al., "Quantum-enhanced Markov chain Monte Carlo", Nature 619, 282–287, published 2023-07-12, DOI 10.1038/s41586-023-06095-4 (arXiv 2203.12497) — [Nature](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06095-4); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.12497)
- Algorithm: the current state |s⟩ is evolved under H = (1 − γ)α H_prob + γ H_mix with H_mix = Σ X_j, measured, and the result is accepted or rejected by Metropolis-Hastings. γ ~ uniform[0.25, 0.6] and t ~ uniform[2, 20] are drawn at random every step and were not optimized. H is symmetric, so U = Uᵀ and the proposal is symmetric, Q(s′|s) = Q(s|s′) — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.12497)
- Average-case model: 500 random instances per n with J_jk and h_j i.i.d. N(0,1), fully connected (SK with random local fields; the fields break inversion symmetry and "increase the complexity"), n from 3 to 10. Exact transition matrices were computed — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.12497)
- Metric: the absolute spectral gap δ of the full M-H transition matrix, against T and n — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.12497)
- Fitted scaling at T = 1 (⟨δ⟩ ∝ 2^(−kn)): quantum k = 0.264(4); mismatched quantum k = 0.76(1); local k = 0.94(4); uniform k = 0.948(7). Quantum enhancement factor in k = 3.6(1). The ratios "suggest a roughly cubic/quartic enhancement at low temperatures regardless of the exact T" — [arXiv PDF, Fig. 2c](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.12497)
- Temperature dependence: at high T, where the target distribution is nearly uniform, the uniform proposal already gives δ near 1. Uniform and local proposals both lose δ sharply at lower T. The quantum algorithm gives "a substantially better δ on average than either classical alternative for T ≲ 1" — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.12497)
- Mechanism: in a perturbative picture, quantum proposals have small |ΔE| ("likely to be accepted even if ΔE > 0"), as local moves do, yet s and s′ are "typically far in Hamming distance, so the resulting Markov chain can move rapidly between distant local energy minima." A "mismatched" quantum proposal that explores the wrong energy landscape performs about as well as uniform, so the gain comes from encoding the actual landscape — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.12497)
- Hardware: ibmq_mumbai (27-qubit superconducting device, Qiskit), up to n = 10 qubits on 1D nearest-neighbour instances with fields, second-order Trotter with up to 48 layers of 2-qubit gates, 5.76×10^7 recorded transitions. The illustrative instances were picked so that the "lowest-E configurations include several near-degenerate local minima — a central feature of spin glasses at larger n which hampers MCMC at low T". In the n = 10 instance the six lowest-E states are all local minima and the two lowest differ by |ΔE| = 0.05 — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.12497)
- Easy instances: on 1D (sparser) instances the local proposal does much better, because "sparser connectivity reduces the potential for frustration, thus increasing the fraction of easy instances". The quantum scaling advantage "persists ... though it is less pronounced" — [arXiv PDF, Supplement](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.12497)
- Noise: errors make the algorithm "more classical"; under fully depolarizing noise it reduces to MCMC with a uniform proposal. The authors say δ cannot be measured at large n and that the combined effect of noise and size is unknown — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.12497)

### Inferences
- The landscape features that drive the Layden advantage are frustration from dense random couplings plus random fields, low temperature, and several near-degenerate low-energy minima far apart in Hamming distance. A smooth, single-peaked landscape removes what the quantum proposal exploits. There a local proposal already mixes fast, and the cost of a quantum proposal cannot pay off.
- The comparison classes were local single-flip and uniform proposals only. Parallel tempering, cluster moves and other strong classical samplers were not in the δ comparison.

### Gaps
- Layden did not report a quantitative ruggedness indicator (for example, number of local minima vs δ gain). The only criteria given are qualitative: near-degenerate distant minima and frustration.

## Q2: Follow-up work 2023–2026, positive and negative results

### Takeaway
Follow-ups split into (a) negative or bounding results: Orfi & Sels show no speedup for unstructured or worst-case problems or for quenches to ergodic (fully thermalizing) dynamics, with performance limited by a delocalization-vs-entropy trade-off. (b) Engineering improvements: QAOA proposals, quantum annealing proposals, coarse-graining, tensor-network "quantum-inspired" proposals, neural surrogates, irreversible proposals, adiabatic dressing. Almost all positive results are on SK or other spin glasses at low temperature, and benchmarks are mostly against local or uniform proposals.

### Cited Findings
- Orfi & Sels, "Bounding the speedup of the quantum-enhanced Markov-chain Monte Carlo algorithm", Phys. Rev. A 110, 052414, 2024-11-12, DOI 10.1103/physreva.110.052414: "there is no speedup over classical sampling on a worst-case unstructured sampling problem". It gives an upper bound on the Markov gap "that rules out a speedup for any unital quantum proposal" — [OpenAlex/PRA](https://doi.org/10.1103/physreva.110.052414)
- Orfi & Sels, "Barriers to efficient mixing of quantum-enhanced Markov chains", Phys. Rev. A 110, 052434, 2024-11-25, DOI 10.1103/physreva.110.052434: the quantum dynamics must "efficiently delocalize the system over a range of classical states", but "it is also detrimental to introduce too much entropy through the quench". In the long-time limit the gap is bounded by the inverse participation ratio of classical states in the eigenstate basis, so there is "no advantage when quenching to an ergodic system". They identify optimal-gap regimes for the SK and three-spin models (abstract-level reading) — [PRA](https://doi.org/10.1103/physreva.110.052434)
- Hsieh, Orfi & Sels, "Adiabatic dressing of quantum-enhanced Markov chains", Phys. Rev. A, 2026-10-13, DOI 10.1103/x255-fw2l: controlling the degree of delocalization by adiabatically dressing the quench "can significantly enhance the Markov gap in paradigmatic spin-glass models" — [PRA](https://doi.org/10.1103/x255-fw2l)
- Christmann, Ivashkov, Chiurco & Mazzola, "From quantum-enhanced to quantum-inspired Monte Carlo", Phys. Rev. A 111, 042615, 2025-04-16, DOI 10.1103/physreva.111.042615: they find "an optimal mixing Hamiltonian strength", analyse how the total evolution time scales with system size, and test time-dependent Hamiltonians and reverse digitized annealing. Tensor-network simulators "even with unconverged settings, can maintain a scaling advantage over standard classical samplers". This means a classical approximate simulation of the quantum proposal can retain much of the gain — [PRA](https://doi.org/10.1103/physreva.111.042615) (abstract only)
- Nakano, Hakoshima, Mitarai & Fujii, "Markov-chain Monte Carlo method enhanced by a quantum alternating operator ansatz", Phys. Rev. Research 6, 033105, 2024-07-25, DOI 10.1103/physrevresearch.6.033105: a QAOA-type proposal that satisfies the symmetry constraint for arbitrary parameters. They report a correlation between the MCMC acceptance rate and the spectral gap, which lets them tune parameters, and they claim a "quadratic speedup in convergence" over uniform proposals on spin glasses — [PRR](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevresearch.6.033105)
- Nakano, Okada & Fujii, "Neural-Network-Assisted Monte Carlo Sampling Trained by QAOA", PRX Quantum, 2026-01-06, DOI 10.1103/9nhx-5pym: a generative neural sampler trained on QAOA samples lifts the symmetry constraint on circuits. On spin glasses it shows a "polynomial speedup (over cubic) in spectral gap compared with uniform proposals" — [PRX Quantum](https://doi.org/10.1103/9nhx-5pym)
- Arai & Kadowaki, "Quantum annealing enhanced Markov-Chain Monte Carlo", Sci. Rep., 2025-07-01, DOI 10.1038/s41598-025-07293-y: on the SK model they report larger spectral gaps, faster energy convergence and lower TVD than classical MCMC — [Sci. Rep.](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-07293-y). An auto-generated review notes the claim may depend on a tuned, oracle-like annealing schedule — [Pith review (aggregator, low weight)](https://pith.science/paper/2502.08060)
- Ferguson & Wallden, "Quantum-enhanced Markov chain Monte Carlo for systems larger than a quantum computer" (coarse-grained QeMCMC), Phys. Rev. Research 7, 013231, 2025-03-03, DOI 10.1103/physrevresearch.7.013231: they coarse-grain the proposal onto subsets of spins, needing about √n simulated qubits. "Only six simulated qubits suffice to gain an advantage ... investigating the magnetization of a 36-spin system" — [PRR](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevresearch.7.013231)
- Cao, Cui, Wang & Tang, "Irreversibility Enhances Quantum-Enhanced Markov-Chain Monte Carlo", arXiv 2606.23350, 2026-06-22 (preprint): state-dependent proposals that break detailed balance outperform QEMC on spin-glass benchmarks, with a spectral gap that increases with system size — [arXiv via OpenAlex](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2606.23350). An aggregator review cautions that stationarity is "asserted rather than derived" in the visible text — [Pith review (aggregator)](https://pith.science/paper/2606.23350)
- Marshall, Egger, Garn, Schiavello, Brandhofer, Zoufal (IBM), "Quantum-enhanced Markov Chain Monte Carlo for Combinatorial Optimization", arXiv 2602.06171, 2026-02-05: QeMCMC used as an optimizer, with warm-start and parallel tempering, on Maximum Independent Set up to 117 variables on 117 qubits of IBM Heron hardware. They state the asymmetric proposals make it "purely a heuristic". The metric is the median iterations for any replica to reach the global optimum. The baseline is replica-exchange MCMC with uniform 1–5 bit-flip proposals. In the single-shot setting the classical method beat simulated quantum on every instance. They claim only "early evidence of a scaling advantage" — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.06171)
- D'Arcangelo, Javanmard & Pearson, "Quantum Markov chain Monte Carlo method with programmable quantum simulators", Phys. Rev. A, 2026-04-13, DOI 10.1103/7y57-1x19: the proposals use a many-body-localized phase. Tuning across the thermalizing-to-localized transition controls the acceptance rate — [PRA](https://doi.org/10.1103/7y57-1x19). This is consistent with Orfi & Sels: fully ergodic quenches are bad, and localized ones keep proposals near the current state.
- Eder et al. (Katzgraber group), "Quantum-Guided Cluster Algorithms for Combinatorial Optimization", IEEE QCE 2025, DOI 10.1109/qce65121.2025.00033: clusters are built from two-point correlations (QAOA or classical), and "flipping induces large transitions in configuration space with high acceptance probability - even at low energy levels". They note standard cluster algorithms have percolation problems on spin glasses — [IEEE QCE](https://doi.org/10.1109/qce65121.2025.00033)

### Inferences
- Taken together, advantage needs a structured, rugged, low-temperature landscape plus a quench in an intermediate regime: delocalized enough to jump between basins, not so ergodic that it becomes a uniform proposal. On a smooth single-basin landscape the best proposal is local, and no paper reports a quantum benefit there.
- Christmann et al. and the neural-surrogate papers show that the "quantum" proposal can partly be emulated classically. A fair comparison should therefore include strong classical non-local proposals, not just single-flip Metropolis.

### Gaps
- I did not find a paper that explicitly reports QeMCMC advantage disappearing on smooth or convex landscapes as a headline result. The evidence is indirect: Layden's 1D/sparse results, the high-T results, and the Orfi & Sels bounds.
- The "Arai/Ferguson/Wild" grouping in the brief: I found Arai & Kadowaki (QA-enhanced MCMC) and Ferguson & Wallden (CGQeMCMC). I found no QeMCMC paper with an author named Wild.
- I did not find a paper on continuous-time QeMCMC.
- Christmann et al.'s numerical optimal γ and time-scaling values were not retrieved (abstract only).

## Q3: QeMCMC or quantum-proposal MCMC for portfolio, finance, or cardinality-constrained QUBOs

### Takeaway
I found no paper that applies QeMCMC to portfolio optimization. The closest work is Kawamata, Nakano & Fujii (PR Applied 2026) and Kawamata & Fujii (arXiv Nov 2026), on fixed-Hamming-weight (cardinality) Boltzmann sampling. They use QAOA with an XY mixer on subblocks, then a weight-conditioned neural surrogate, against local and global Kawasaki (swap) dynamics. That swap baseline is the same as our swap neighbourhood.

### Cited Findings
- Kawamata, Nakano & Fujii, "Divide-and-conquer neural-network surrogates for quantum sampling: Accelerating MCMC in large-scale constrained optimization problems", Phys. Rev. Applied, 2026-11-09, DOI 10.1103/qprn-rs96: MCMC "under fixed Hamming weight constraints". The interaction graph is split into subgraphs, QAOA with an XY mixer generates samples, and neural surrogates "conditioned on the Hamming weight" provide proposals "while preserving the constraint". On 3-regular graphs the autocorrelation decay rate improves by about 14.0× over local and 7.7× over global Kawasaki dynamics, with the gain growing in N. On MNIST feature-mask selection (N = 784) they report 2.03 percentage points higher accuracy than global Kawasaki after 50 steps — [PR Applied](https://doi.org/10.1103/qprn-rs96)
- Kawamata & Fujii, "Hardware Demonstration of Neural-Surrogated QeMCMC for Large-Scale Constrained Boltzmann Sampling", arXiv 2611.05883, 2026-11-04 (preprint): QUBO with Q_ij ~ N(0,1) on IBM heavy-hex graphs, half filling K = N/2 (K = 72 for N = 144; K = 532 for N = 1064), β = 0.5–1.2 — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.05883)
  - Constraint handling: XY mixer H_M = ½ Σ(X_iX_j + Y_iY_j) preserves Hamming weight, with initial state |+⟩ (not a Dicke state), so samples span weight sectors. A conditional MADE is trained with block weight as context. Proposed block configurations whose weight does not match are rejected; valid ones go through M-H on the full energy — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.05883)
  - Baselines: global Kawasaki (swap any 1-bit with any 0-bit) and local Kawasaki (swap adjacent vertices only), both symmetric — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.05883)
  - Results: N = 1064 at β = 0.5, decay rate about 5.2× global and 12.3× local Kawasaki; N = 144, about 4.71× and 9.95×. Limitations: a single instance at one temperature; mixing deteriorates for larger blocks; invalid-weight proposals rise with block size; mixing "generally deteriorates" as the target is cooled — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.05883)
- Marshall et al. 2026 (IBM) handled the MIS constraint with a penalty term λH_constraint and a transverse-field (X) mixer, not a constraint-preserving mixer. They did not cover portfolio or cardinality instances — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.06171)
- Constraint-preserving building blocks from QAOA (not MCMC) work: XY mixers restrict evolution to the fixed-Hamming-weight subspace — [Constrained QAOA for extractive summarization, arXiv 2206.06290](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.06290). He et al. (npj QI 2023) ran constrained portfolio tests with N = 6 assets and K = 3. Performance was best when the initial state was the ground state of the mixer (complete-XY "aligned" vs ring-XY "misaligned") — [npj QI](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41534-023-00787-5). Dicke-state initialization plus an XY mixer that "strictly preserves the Hamming weight" for portfolios — [Mancilla et al., arXiv 2602.14827](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14827) (abstract only)
- A 2024 paper on index tracking (a cardinality-constrained finance problem) with hybrid simulated annealing cites Layden et al. — [Physica A, DOI 10.1016/j.physa.2024.129637](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physa.2024.129637) (title only via OpenAlex; content not verified)

### Inferences
- How to adapt QeMCMC to choose-k-of-n: the symmetric-proposal condition in Layden needs U = Uᵀ. XY terms (X_iX_j + Y_iY_j) are real-symmetric, and Z-diagonal problem terms are too, so H_prob plus an XY mixer yields a symmetric proposal confined to the weight-k sector. This follows from Layden's own condition, which allows "Xj and YjYk terms". Starting from the current valid portfolio, the quench then gives a constraint-preserving, symmetric, multi-swap proposal. A complete-graph XY mixer matches the global-swap neighbourhood. This is an inference, not a published QeMCMC result.
- For n = 16, k = 5 the feasible space is only C(16,5) = 4368 states. Exact δ is computable classically, so the team can reproduce a Layden-style gap analysis directly.

### Gaps
- No published QeMCMC application to portfolio optimization (Markowitz, cardinality-constrained) found as of November 2026.
- No published study of a pure Layden-style (non-surrogate) QeMCMC with an XY mixer evaluated by exact spectral gap in the fixed-weight subspace was found.

## Q4: Instance families hard for local/swap Metropolis where quantum proposals help; ruggedness indicators

### Takeaway
Every documented quantum-proposal gain is on frustrated spin glasses at low temperature: SK with random fields, three-spin models, 3-regular or heavy-hex random-coupling QUBOs, and MIS graphs. The only instance-level predictor actually reported is having several near-degenerate local minima far apart in Hamming distance, along with lower connectivity or frustration making instances easier. I found no paper that correlates a scalar ruggedness metric with QeMCMC gain.

### Cited Findings
- SK with random fields, T ≲ 1 — [Layden et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.12497); SK and three-spin models, optimal regimes linked to eigenstate properties (IPR) — [Orfi & Sels, Barriers](https://doi.org/10.1103/physreva.110.052434)
- Illustrative hard instances were chosen to have multiple near-degenerate low-energy local minima (n = 10: the six lowest states are local minima; the gap between the lowest two is 0.05) — [Layden et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.12497)
- 1D sparse instances have less frustration, so the local proposal does much better and the quantum advantage shrinks — [Layden et al. Supplement](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.12497)
- Fixed-weight QUBOs with Gaussian couplings on 3-regular or heavy-hex graphs beat Kawasaki swap dynamics — [Kawamata et al. PR Applied](https://doi.org/10.1103/qprn-rs96); [Kawamata & Fujii arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.05883)
- Spin glasses with disorder and frustration trap single-spin-flip simulated annealing in local minima, and cluster algorithms percolate on them — [Eder et al. QCE 2025](https://doi.org/10.1109/qce65121.2025.00033)
- Orfi & Sels give a theoretical quantity: the inverse participation ratio of computational states in the H eigenbasis bounds the long-time gap — [PRA 110, 052434](https://doi.org/10.1103/physreva.110.052434)

### Inferences
- Indicators the team can compute on its own landscapes (inferred from the mechanism, not validated in the literature): the number of local optima under the swap neighbourhood; the Hamming distance between the global optimum and the next-best local optima; the energy gap between them relative to T; the energy barrier along swap paths between basins; and the exact δ for local swap vs uniform-feasible proposals. If local swap δ is already near the uniform δ, or the landscape has a single basin, no proposal can beat it materially.
- To make a finance instance QeMCMC-friendly, the objective needs frustration. Examples: Σ with strong mixed-sign correlations (hedging pairs and clusters), a small or negative-heavy return-risk balance so the λ·xᵀΣx term dominates, or added competing pairwise terms such as sector caps or transaction costs. The aim is to create several near-degenerate portfolios that differ by many swaps. A low sampling temperature relative to the score spread is also required.

### Gaps
- Planted spin glasses, Wishart planted ensembles, frustrated-loop instances and Ising chains with tunable barriers: I found no QeMCMC paper benchmarking on these families. They are plausible candidates but undocumented for QeMCMC.
- No literature-validated quantitative ruggedness threshold that predicts QeMCMC advantage.

## Q5: Metrics for fairly demonstrating advantage

### Takeaway
For small systems use the exact absolute spectral gap of the full transition matrix, as in Layden, Orfi & Sels, Nakano, and Arai. For larger ones use autocorrelation decay or TVD and energy convergence to the Boltzmann distribution. For optimization, use iterations or samples to reach the global optimum. Baselines must include strong non-local classical proposals, and the cost per step (shots, circuit time) must be reported.

### Cited Findings
- The absolute spectral gap δ is "a broad and unambiguous figure of merit, but it is not feasible to measure for large instances" — [Layden et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.12497)
- Arai & Kadowaki report the spectral gap, energy-observable convergence and the TVD between empirical and target distributions — [Sci. Rep.](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-07293-y)
- Kawamata & Fujii report the replica-pair autocorrelation decay rate per MCMC step, not the spectral gap — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.05883)
- Marshall et al. report median iterations until a replica reaches the global optimum, and fit scaling across sizes. Wall-clock time is not the primary metric — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.06171)
- Nakano et al. use the acceptance rate as an experimentally measurable proxy that correlates with the gap in a parameter range — [PRR](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevresearch.6.033105)

### Inferences
- A fair protocol for the team's setting: (1) exact δ on the 4368-state feasible space for local swap, global swap (uniform-feasible) and the quantum XY proposal, swept over T; (2) the TVD to the exact Boltzmann distribution against MCMC steps; (3) for optimization, evaluations to the global optimum, with attempts, score evaluations, shots and wall-clock time logged separately (per project conventions). Include parallel tempering or global-swap baselines, since most published "advantages" are only against local or uniform proposals.

### Gaps
- No consensus "fair cost model" in the literature that converts quantum shots or circuit time to classical evaluations. Most papers compare per-step metrics only.
