# Engineer frustration before chasing quantum MCMC advantage

The current 5-of-16 ETF mean-variance problem cannot show a QeMCMC advantage, and that is expected rather than a bug: with the return term about 13 times larger than the covariance couplings and a dominant market factor, swap moves see a nearly linear objective, and a linear objective over k-subsets has exactly one swap-local optimum. Every published quantum-proposal gain comes from frustrated, dense, low-temperature spin-glass landscapes whose best states are near-degenerate and far apart in Hamming distance ([Layden et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.12497)). The finance problems most likely to reproduce that regime, ranked by fit, are (1) cardinality-constrained minimum variance or residual mean-variance with μ shrunk until pairwise terms dominate, on a mixed-asset universe; (2) market-neutral long/short books with exact long and short counts, which have the strongest theoretical support for exponentially many quasi-degenerate optima ([Galluccio, Bouchaud & Potters 1998](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/9801209)); (3) max-diversification, least-correlated-k or k-independent-set selection on an asset correlation graph; (4) credit-scoring feature selection on German Credit, the best "banking" story with prior quantum work; and (5) a planted, factor-neutral semi-synthetic family with a known optimum and a single hardness dial. No paper applies QeMCMC to portfolio selection as of November 2026, so a team that shows a frustrated finance instance where a weight-preserving quantum proposal beats strong classical samplers would fill a real gap. The claim must be narrow, though: exact spectral gaps at n = 16 are a fixed-size proposal-efficiency result, theory rules out speedups on unstructured problems ([Orfi & Sels 2024](https://doi.org/10.1103/physreva.110.052414)), and parallel tempering, uniform k-subset proposals and classical emulations of the quantum proposal are the baselines most likely to erase any apparent gain.

## A linear field 13 times stronger than the couplings guarantees one peak

The single-peak result follows from a short argument. On the space of k-subsets with swap moves, a purely linear score Σ cᵢxᵢ has exactly one local optimum, the top-k set: any other set holds some asset i and omits some asset j with cⱼ > cᵢ, and swapping them improves the score. Because xᵢ² = xᵢ for binary variables, the diagonal of Σ folds into the linear term, so the effective field on asset i is hᵢ = μᵢ/k − λΣᵢᵢ/k². Ruggedness can only appear when the off-diagonal couplings λΣᵢⱼ/k² vary across pairs by an amount comparable to the spread of hᵢ. The team's measured **13:1 ratio of linear to pairwise magnitudes puts the problem deep in the sorting regime**, so all 64 quarterly windows being single-peaked is the predicted outcome.

The market factor makes the couplings even less useful. Under a one-factor model Σ = σm²ββᵀ + D, the equal-weight quadratic term becomes σm²(β·x)² plus a separable part, a convex function of one aggregate. That adds no frustration. More subtly, **under a fixed cardinality the uniform part of the market mode is a constant**: if all betas were equal, β·x = kβ̄ for every valid portfolio. Only beta dispersion survives, and it acts like a rank-one "prefer low beta" pull that favors one coherent cluster, a ferromagnetic rather than glassy effect. Removing the market mode therefore mostly rescales the off-diagonal spread without creating mixed-sign competition, and it leaves the 13:1 ratio from μ untouched. The planned "remove the market mode step by step" dial should be kept as an ablation, with the prediction that on its own it does little.

The only direct landscape measurement in the operations research literature agrees. On the OR-Library S&P 100 instance, steepest-descent swap search found **one local minimum in most settings and at most five**, with the global basin covering 30–60% of the search space, and the author noted that this smoothness appeared to be "a common feature in financial market related instances" ([di Tollo 2015](https://annals-csis.org/Volume_5/pliks/7.pdf)). Ruggedness appeared only when di Tollo changed the encoding to continuous weights, or built an artificial covariance of strongly anti-correlated hedge pairs (off-diagonal −1 and −0.9), where every hedged pair became a local optimum and the global basin shrank as 1/n. The quantum portfolio literature has not looked for hardness: Hodson et al. used 8 ASX stocks ([arXiv 1911.05296](https://arxiv.org/html/1911.05296)) and Brandhofer et al. likely DAX 30 ([arXiv 2207.10555](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.10555)), and neither reports local-optima structure.

Contrast that with what QeMCMC needs. In Layden et al.'s exact spectral-gap study on fully connected SK instances with random fields, the quantum proposal's gap scaled as roughly 2^(−0.264n) at T = 1, against about 2^(−0.94n) for single-flip and 2^(−0.948n) for uniform proposals, a **roughly cubic-to-quartic improvement concentrated at T ≲ 1** ([Layden et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.12497)). The mechanism is proposals that are far in Hamming distance but small in |ΔE|, so the chain jumps between distant minima and is still accepted. A "mismatched" quantum proposal built on the wrong landscape did no better than uniform, and sparse 1D instances, with less frustration, shrank the advantage. A single-basin landscape removes everything the quantum proposal exploits: local moves already mix fast there, and no paper reports a quantum benefit on such landscapes.

## Five finance formulations can create the frustration QeMCMC needs

Ruggedness requires pairwise couplings that are (a) not close to rank one, (b) mixed in sign or heterogeneous enough that the cardinality constraint forces competition, and (c) at least comparable in spread to the linear field. The table ranks candidates by expected ruggedness under swap moves, fit to an exact-k quadratic encoding at n ≈ 12–20, and how much of the existing pipeline they reuse. The ranking is the team's reasoning built from the cited evidence. No published study counts swap local optima for any of these at this size, so each must pass the enumeration test below before QeMCMC runs.

| Rank | Formulation | Source of frustration | Order / size | Evidence strength | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cardinality-constrained minimum variance, or residual mean-variance with μ shrunk, on a mixed-asset 16-ETF universe | Hedge pairs (equity vs Treasuries, gold, USD, volatility) give attractive couplings; redundant pairs repulsive | Quadratic; 4,368 states at 5 of 16 | Inferred from mechanism plus di Tollo's hedge-pair instance | Stock–bond sign is regime-dependent, so windows vary |
| 2 | Market-neutral long/short, exactly k_L longs and k_S shorts | Effective coupling σᵢⱼ·zᵢzⱼ flips sign with position; optima driven by C⁻¹ | Quadratic; 2,970 states at 2+2 of 12, 160,160 at 3+3 of 16 | Strongest theory: exponentially many quasi-degenerate optima | Count result is for continuous weights, not exact-k swaps |
| 3 | Max-diversification / least-correlated k / k-independent set on a thresholded correlation graph | Cardinality constraint frustrates all-repulsive heterogeneous couplings | Quadratic; reuses ETF data | Large metaheuristic literature; densest-k-subgraph inapproximable | Small-n optima counts unknown |
| 4 | Credit-scoring feature selection (German Credit, k of 20) | Relevance vs redundancy; mixed signs only with signed or conditional-MI couplings | Quadratic; 38,760 states at 6 of 20 | Several quantum QUBO papers, no landscape statistics | Plain MI-redundancy QUBO may be smooth |
| 5 | Planted factor-neutral portfolio (Wishart-style Σ = WWᵀ + D) | Tunable via number of factors per asset | Quadratic; any n | Wishart planted ensemble has easy-hard-easy profile | Adaptation to exact-k untested; planted structure can leak |
| 6 | k-medoids stress-scenario selection, p-median branch/ATM siting | Min-over-sites objective | Higher-order or needs auxiliary qubits | Strongest classical multimodality evidence (PLS-complete local search) | Poor hardware fit; simulation only |

**Rank 1 is the cheapest experiment and the most credible finance story.** Setting μ to zero gives cardinality-constrained minimum variance, a standard problem for an investor who distrusts noisy mean estimates; shrinking μ with James-Stein-type methods or raising λ is the continuous version of the same dial. The universe matters more than residualization: a mixed-asset set such as SPY, EFA, EEM, IWM, TLT, IEF, SHY, TIP, LQD, HYG, GLD, SLV, DBC, UUP, VIXY and VNQ adds hedge pairs that behave like di Tollo's artificial instance, which he described as hard to find in real markets but "not forbidden by structural properties" ([di Tollo 2015](https://annals-csis.org/Volume_5/pliks/7.pdf)). The research did not verify correlation signs for this list, so each window must be checked rather than assumed.

**Rank 2 has the best theoretical grounding.** Galluccio, Bouchaud and Potters showed that portfolios with long and short positions under a nonlinear margin constraint reduce to TAP equations of a spin glass with couplings proportional to C⁻¹, which is mixed in sign even when C is all positive. They found that "the number of 'optimal' solutions is generically exponentially large", that a 100-stock US portfolio can be built "in ∼10²⁹ different ways", and that quasi-degenerate solutions "can be very far from each other" ([Galluccio et al. 1998](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/9801209)). Far-apart, near-degenerate optima are exactly the Layden regime. Long-only portfolios without cardinality also show a spin-glass-like landscape with disorder chaos ([Garnier-Brun et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.00668)). Hodson et al.'s ternary encoding with separate XY mixers on long and short registers is a ready-made constraint-preserving design ([arXiv 1911.05296](https://arxiv.org/html/1911.05296)). Starting at n = 12 with two longs and two shorts keeps the state space at 2,970, comparable to the current 4,368.

**Rank 3 keeps the data and changes the objective.** Picking k of 16 ETFs to maximize pairwise correlation distance is the Maximum Diversity Problem, whose benchmark library (MDPLIB, 315 instances) and comparison of 30 heuristics show that plain local search is not enough ([Martí et al. 2013](https://grafo.etsii.urjc.es/en/publication/marti-2013-heuristics); [Wu & Hao 2013](https://leria-info.univ-angers.fr/%7Ejinkao.hao/papers/EJORWuHao2013.pdf)). The related densest-k-subgraph problem is hard to approximate within almost-polynomial ratios under ETH ([Manurangsi 2017](https://doi.org/10.1145/3055399.3055412)). Thresholding the correlation matrix into a graph and asking for a k-independent set connects directly to IBM's 2026 QeMCMC work on Maximum Independent Set, which names financial services as a motivation ([Marshall et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06171)).

**Rank 4 is the strongest banking narrative but needs a ruggedness check.** Credit feature selection as a k-of-n QUBO balancing relevance against redundancy has a lineage from 1QBit on UCI German Credit ([Milne et al. 2017](https://1qbit.com/whitepaper/optimal-feature-selection-in-credit-scoring-classification-using-quantum-annealer/)) through Mücke et al. ([arXiv 2203.13261](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.13261)), IBM's VarQFS ([Zoufal et al. 2023](https://quantum-journal.org/papers/q-2023-01-26-909/)) and American Express ([Samanta et al. 2025](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI-SS/article/view/36910)). It also has a natural sampling motive: a distribution over near-optimal feature subsets supports importance stability and model averaging. The catch is that a plain mutual-information redundancy QUBO has all-repulsive couplings, so its frustration comes only from the constraint. Signed correlations or conditional-MI synergy terms add genuine sign mixing. None of these papers reports landscape statistics, so the team must enumerate 6-of-20 (38,760 states) before investing.

**Rank 5 gives a controllable stress test.** The Wishart planted ensemble defines an Ising energy ‖Wᵀs‖² with a planted ground state and shows a pronounced easy-hard-easy profile in α = M/N, peaking at small α (three columns at N = 32) and becoming an easy Curie-Weiss ferromagnet at large α ([Hamze et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.00275)). Read through finance, W holds factor loadings and the planted state is a portfolio with zero exposure to all M factors, which suggests a "find the factor-neutral 5-of-16 basket" instance with one to three factors. This adaptation to exact-k 0/1 selection is untested, and a 2026 audit warns planted benchmarks can leak structure ([Li 2026](https://doi.org/10.1109/access.2026.3738535)), so treat it as a control family, not the headline.

Several popular finance add-ons do not help. Fixed transaction costs and turnover are linear in binary holdings and shift the optimum without adding frustration; sector quotas split the swap graph without creating competing couplings; Sharpe ratio and index tracking against a broad index stay market-dominated. Facility location and k-medoids have the best classical evidence of many swap optima (p-median local search is tight PLS-complete, [Alekseeva & Kochetov 2008](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/ejores/v191y2008i3p736-752.html)), but their min-over-sites objective is higher-order, so they suit classical simulation of a diagonal phase, not hardware. Collateral, settlement and loan-pool selection use inequality budgets rather than exact counts ([Giron et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16395); [Braine et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1109/tqe.2021.3063635)) and fit poorly at this size.

## The coupling-to-field ratio is the master dial

All tuning knobs act through one dimensionless quantity, the spread of pairwise couplings relative to the spread of the effective field:

$$
r = \frac{(\lambda/k^2)\cdot \mathrm{spread}_{i<j}(\Sigma_{ij})}{\mathrm{spread}_i(h_i)}, \qquad h_i = \frac{\mu_i}{k} - \frac{\lambda\,\Sigma_{ii}}{k^2}
$$

Spread rather than mean is what matters, because any uniform part of Σ is constant under the cardinality constraint. **Multiple optima should appear only once r reaches roughly order one**; the current problem sits far below that. Reporting r per window is cheaper and more interpretable than the 13:1 magnitude ratio. This threshold is an inference from the linear-objective argument, not a published constant, which is why the first experiment sweeps it.

Four secondary knobs move r or the sign structure. First, **risk-return balance**: raise λ or shrink μ toward its cross-sectional mean, down to μ = 0. Second, **estimation noise**: shorter windows of L daily returns, with n/L approaching 1, inject Marchenko-Pastur noise eigenvectors with random-sign loadings and make couplings more SK-like, while Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage or RMT cleaning smooths the landscape ([Laloux et al. 1999](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevlett.83.1467)). Galluccio et al. tie the number of optima to exactly this random-matrix part of C, and found that large eigenvalues suppress metastable states ([Galluccio et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/9801209)). The finance reading is honest: the optimizer's landscape is rugged because estimates are noisy. Third, **universe composition** and **position signs** create attractive and repulsive pairs together, the multi-funnel structure that local optima network studies link to search difficulty ([Ochoa & Veerapen 2018](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s10732-017-9334-0)). Fourth, for planted instances, the **number of factors per asset** plays the role of the Wishart α.

Temperature is the knob most often misused. Hardness exists only relative to the sampling temperature: far above the barrier every proposal mixes, and far below it nothing does. Layden et al. saw the quantum advantage only at T ≲ 1 relative to their coupling scale ([Layden et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.12497)), and the Wishart ensemble shows first-order transitions at low T that trap even parallel tempering ([Hamze et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.00275)). The team should report full δ(T) curves and choose the benchmark temperature where the classical swap gap is small but not numerically zero, comparable to the barrier height and to the gap between the best and second-best scores.

An adaptive instance search can also raise hardness by orders of magnitude. Marshall, Martin-Mayor and Hen mutated coupling signs and kept changes that slowed solvers, making instances about 10 times harder than random after 500 steps and the hardest about 250 times harder ([Marshall et al. 2016](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1605.03607)). Applied to finance parameters (λ, window, universe, shrinkage), this is powerful but biased: instances selected to slow swap Metropolis are cherry-picked against that one baseline unless every other baseline is run on them too.

## Exact spectral gaps make n = 16 a clean testbed

The feasible spaces are small enough to compute everything exactly, which few QeMCMC studies can claim. The primary metric is the **absolute spectral gap δ = 1 − max |λ| of the full transition matrix**, swept over T, with relaxation time 1/δ; Layden et al. call it "a broad and unambiguous figure of merit" that is infeasible only at large n ([Layden et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.12497)). At 4,368 or 2,970 states, dense eigensolvers work; at 38,760 or 160,160, sparse ones do.

Three landscape statistics explain the gap and serve as a cheap screen before any quantum run. The first is the **number of strict swap-local optima and the global optimum's basin share**. The second is the **barrier B**, the minimum over paths from the second-best optimum to the global one of the largest score drop, computed by a union-find sweep over states sorted by score, which also yields a disconnectivity graph; standard large-deviation reasoning predicts 1/δ grows roughly like exp(B/T) at low temperature, making B/T the most promising scalar predictor of slow swap mixing. The third is the **score gap ΔS and Hamming distance between the best two optima**, since QeMCMC's mechanism needs near-degenerate optima far apart. A Boltzmann-weighted overlap histogram at the benchmark T is a useful companion: bimodality signals two separated clusters. Fitness-distance correlation and Weinberger autocorrelation length are weak here because a quadratic function on the k-subset (Johnson) graph decomposes into very few elementary components, so they should appear only as context ([Chicano, landscape theory](https://neo.lcc.uma.es/staff/francis/pdf/gecco10-chicano.pdf)). No paper validates any of these against exact gaps on k-subset landscapes, so the team's own correlation of B/T with δ would itself be a contribution.

A practical acceptance rule, set before running QeMCMC, is to keep instances with **at least 3 swap-local optima and a global basin share below 30%**, the bottom of di Tollo's range for easy real instances ([di Tollo 2015](https://annals-csis.org/Volume_5/pliks/7.pdf)). This threshold is heuristic. Publishing it in advance, and reporting the fraction of windows that pass, guards against the selection-bias trap.

For larger or hardware runs, the literature uses complementary metrics: total variation distance to the exact Boltzmann distribution against steps ([Arai & Kadowaki 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-07293-y)), autocorrelation decay rate per step ([Kawamata & Fujii 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.05883)), median iterations for any replica to hit the global optimum ([Marshall et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.06171)), and acceptance rate as a measurable proxy that correlates with the gap over a parameter range ([Nakano et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevresearch.6.033105)). The literature has no agreed model converting shots and circuit time into classical evaluations, so per the project's conventions, attempts, score evaluations, shots and wall-clock time should be logged as separate fields.

## A weight-preserving quantum proposal must beat parallel tempering, not just swaps

The quantum proposal has to respect the cardinality constraint. Layden's symmetric-proposal condition requires U = Uᵀ; XY terms (XᵢXⱼ + YᵢYⱼ) are real-symmetric and Z-diagonal problem terms are too, so evolving the current valid portfolio under H_prob plus a complete-graph XY mixer gives a **symmetric, Hamming-weight-preserving, multi-swap proposal** whose classical analogue is global swap. This follows from Layden's stated condition but has not been published as a QeMCMC result. The closest work is Kawamata, Nakano and Fujii, whose XY-mixer QAOA with neural surrogates improved autocorrelation decay about 14 times over local and 7.7 times over global Kawasaki (swap) dynamics on fixed-weight 3-regular instances ([PR Applied 2026](https://doi.org/10.1103/qprn-rs96)), and about 5.2 times over global Kawasaki at N = 1064 on IBM hardware, though on a single instance at one temperature, with mixing degrading as the target cooled ([arXiv 2611.05883](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.05883)). IBM's own MIS work used a penalty and a transverse-field mixer instead, called the asymmetric result "purely a heuristic", and found the classical replica-exchange baseline beat simulated quantum on every instance in the single-shot setting ([Marshall et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.06171)). A plain X-mixer proposal on this problem would leak out of the weight-k sector and pay a rejection overhead, so the XY design is the one to build.

Baselines decide whether any gap ratio means anything. Layden et al. compared only against local and uniform proposals, and Swendsen-Wang, Wolff and Houdayer cluster moves gave almost no improvement at their sizes ([Layden et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2203.12497)). A credible portfolio study needs, at matched target distribution, temperature and attempt budget: local swap Metropolis; a **uniform random k-subset proposal**, which at 4,368 states can reach anything in one step; multi-swap and heat-bath proposals; **parallel tempering with swap moves**, budget summed across replicas, the standard rugged-landscape method and the one most likely to erase a gain ([Hukushima & Nemoto 1996](https://doi.org/10.1143/jpsj.65.1604)); simulated annealing; tabu search and greedy restarts as the practical portfolio heuristics; and exact enumeration as ground truth. Two controls matter specifically for the quantum claim. A **mismatched quantum proposal** built on the wrong landscape tests whether gains come from encoding the problem, and a **classically simulated quantum proposal** (exact statevector at this size, or tensor networks) tests whether the advantage is quantum at all, since Christmann et al. found tensor-network emulations "can maintain a scaling advantage over standard classical samplers" ([Christmann et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1103/physreva.111.042615)).

Theory sets the ceiling. Orfi and Sels proved no speedup on worst-case unstructured sampling for any unital quantum proposal, and showed that quenches which fully thermalize reduce to a uniform proposal, so the useful regime is an intermediate one, delocalized enough to jump basins but not ergodic ([Orfi & Sels, Bounding](https://doi.org/10.1103/physreva.110.052414); [Orfi & Sels, Barriers](https://doi.org/10.1103/physreva.110.052434)). Layden's γ ~ uniform[0.25, 0.6] and t ~ uniform[2, 20] were not optimized, and later work found an optimal mixing strength ([Christmann et al.](https://doi.org/10.1103/physreva.111.042615)) and gains from adiabatic dressing ([Hsieh, Orfi & Sels 2026](https://doi.org/10.1103/x255-fw2l)), so sweeping γ and t is part of the experiment. Noise pushes the proposal toward uniform ([Layden et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.12497)), so hardware runs at this size test feasibility, not advantage.

The experiment sequence follows from this. **E0**, a half-day diagnostic: compute r, optima count, basin share, B and ΔS for all 64 existing windows to confirm r is far below 1. **E1**: on the same 16 ETFs, sweep r from the current value through μ = 0 and plot optima count, B, and swap-versus-uniform δ(T); include the market-mode removal as an ablation. **E2**: repeat E1 on the mixed-asset universe and sweep window length L from about n to 4n daily returns, recording what fraction of windows pass the acceptance rule. **E3**: market-neutral long/short with two longs and two shorts from 12, then three and three from 16. **E4**: max-diversification and k-independent set on the ETF correlation graph. **E5**: German Credit feature selection at 6 of 20, with plain MI and with signed or conditional couplings. **E6**: on the instances that pass, build the exact XY-mixer QeMCMC transition matrix, sweep γ, t and T, and compare δ against every baseline including the mismatched and classically emulated controls. **E7**: scale the winning formulation to 6 of 20 to show a trend, and only then run small hardware chains with acceptance rate and TVD as the measurable metrics.

## Conclusion

The project's null result is informative: it shows that the textbook finance problem sits in the one regime where QeMCMC has nothing to exploit, and it points to the precise quantity, the coupling-to-field ratio r, that separates sorting problems from glassy ones. That turns the research question from "which finance problem favors quantum MCMC?" into "which economically defensible choices (distrusting mean estimates, holding hedges, running long/short books, using short noisy windows) push r above one and create far-apart near-degenerate optima?" Each of those choices has a real investor story, which makes the resulting instances more than synthetic spin glasses wearing finance labels.

The most valuable deliverable at this scale is not a speedup headline but a map: for each formulation and knob setting, the exact optima count, barrier and gap for swap, uniform, parallel tempering and XY-quantum proposals across temperature. No published work provides that map for any cardinality-constrained finance problem, and no published QeMCMC study uses an exact-gap analysis in the fixed-weight subspace. If the quantum proposal wins only where parallel tempering also struggles and a classical emulation does not, that is a credible, publishable fixed-size result; if parallel tempering always matches it, the team will have shown where the ceiling sits, which the field also lacks.
